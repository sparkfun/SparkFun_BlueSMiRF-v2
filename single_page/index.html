    Introduction

    Introduction

    • Our BlueSMiRF product line is a series of wireless Bluetooth® serial links. The SparkFun BlueSMiRF v2 is a reboot of the previous BlueSMiRF board, utilizing the Espressif's ESP32 Pico Mini for wireless communication and comes in several variants, with PTH pads, male header pins, or a JST connector. These boards operate as a serial data link and are a great replacement for hardwired between boards. Simply connect a BlueSMiRF v2, pair the devices over Bluetooth®, and simultaneously transmit and receive data! Any serial stream from 2400 - 921600bps can be passed between two devices; with a range of 100' (33m). In this tutorial, we'll go over the hardware and the connections to a BlueSMiRF v2. We will also provide a few examples; including, how to connect to the BlueSMiRF v2 with a smartphone and a basic Arduino example transmitting data between two BlueSMiRF v2s... so let's get started!

    Revision History

    The Original BlueSMiRF

    Back in April 2005, we released the original BlueSMiRF (Bluetooth Serial Miniature RF) Link, which featured BlueRadio's WML-C40AH Bluetooth modems. It was released in two variants that came with either a 4-pin header on the basic BlueSMiRF or a 6-pin header on the extended BlueSMiRF, using the following pin configuration (CTS)/PWR/GND/TX-O/RX-I/(RTS).

    Silver, Gold, and Mate

    Eventually, the Bluetooth modems manufactured by BlueRadio were discontinued and we transitioned to the RN-41 and RN-42 modules from Roving Network.

    Gold/Silver Variants

    The primary difference between the RN-41 and RN-42 modules was their range/transmit power. The BlueSMiRF Gold featured the RN-41, a class 1 Bluetooth module that had a range of 100m, but utilized more power. Meanwhile, the BlueSMiRF Silver featured the RN-42, a class 2 module with lower power consumption and a range limited to 10m.

    Mate Header

    With this revision, we also settled on a 6-pin layout to breakout the flow control pins of the serial interface. Additionally, in response to the growing popularity of miniaturized development boards, like the Arduino Pro and Pro Mini, we introduced a new variant with a compatible pin layout. The BlueSMiRF Mate variants had a pin layout ( GND/CTS/VCC/TXO/RXI/RTS) that matched the USB-to-Serial adapters that were used to program and interface with the development boards. This allowed users to easily extend the capabilities of their board with the BlueSMiRF Mate.

    The Next Generation (v2)

    Just as the Bluetooth modems from BlueRadio were discontinued, production of the modules from Roving Network also ceased and in 2021 we sold the last of the BlueSMiRF Gold and Silver. Fast forward to January 2024 and the BlueSMiRF has arisen once more! The new BlueSMiRFs v2 features a few upgrades:

    • Features an ESP32-PICO-MINI-02 module programmed with our open-source firmware
      • Easily update the firmware Over-the-Air or through the serial interface
      • Remotely configure the settings with a simple AT-command set
    • We have three variants with different connections to simplify hooking up the BlueSMiRF to your project
      • The PTH pad and male header variants have fully, adopted the pin layout from the Bluetooth Mate
        • RTS/RXI/TXO/VCC/CTS/GND
        • Pin labels are now provided on both sides of the board
      • On the JST variant, the pin layout and JST connector matches the serial radios
    • A user button has been added to simplify pairing, triggering various modes of operation, and resetting the factory defaults
    • Upgraded the voltage regulator to the APA2112K 3.3V, 600mA for increased performance

    ESP32 Pico-Mini

    The brains of the BlueSMiRF v2 is an ESP32 Pico-Mini module. The Espressif series of ESP32 modules are a versatile, compact MCU modules with WiFi, BT, and BLE capabilities that target a wide variety of applications. At the core of the ESP32 Pico-Mini module is the ESP32-PICO-V3-02, a System-in-Package device, along with an integrated 8 MB SPI flash, 2 MB SPI PSRAM, and 40 MHz oscillator. While the ESP32 Pico-Mini module has twenty-seven multifunctional GPIO that are available, only seven of I/O pins are actually utilized on the BlueSMiRF v2.

    Note

    The ESP32 module can get a little warm when operating; don't be alarmed, this is just heat dissipating from the internal components.

    Warning

    Users should be aware of the following nuances of the ESP32 Pico-Mini module:

    • All the GPIO are 3.3V pins.
      • The I/O pins are not 5V-tolerant! To interface with higher voltage components, a logic level adapter is recommended.
    • The ESP32 Pico-Mini is only compatible with 2.4GHz WiFi networks; it will not work on the 5GHz bands.
    • There are electrical limitations to the amount of current that the ESP32 Pico-Mini module can sink or source. For more details, check out the ESP32 Pico-Mini module datasheet.
    • There are some limitations to the ADC performance, see the Note from the ADC Characteristics section of the ESP32 SoC datasheet.
    • For details on the boot mode configuration, please refer to section 3.3 Strapping Pins of the ESP32 SoC datasheet.
    Power Modes

    The ESP32 Pico-Mini module has various power modes:

    • Active - The chip radio is powered on. The chip can receive, transmit, or listen.
    • Modem Sleep - The CPU is operational and the clock is configurable. The Wi-Fi/Bluetooth baseband and radio are disabled.
    • Light Sleep - The CPU is paused. The RTC memory and RTC peripherals, as well as the ULP coprocessor are running.
    • Deep Sleep - Only the RTC memory and RTC peripherals are powered on. The ULP coprocessor is functional.
    • Hibernation - Only one RTC timer on the slow clock and certain RTC GPIO active.
    • Off - Chip is powered off.

    For more information on the power management of the ESP32 Pico-Mini module, pleaser refer to Section 4.4 and Tables: 8 and 9 of the ESP32 Pico-Mini module datasheet.

    Firmware

    Firmware

    The BlueSMiRF v2 comes pre-programmed with the latest version of our open-source firmware. However, users are free to modify the firmware and/or reprogram their board for their purposes.

    Our firmware provides short-range, point-to-point communication; allowing serial UART data to be transmitted between two Bluetooth devices. It includes support for both the SPP and BLE protocols and features a massive >10kB buffer for each RX and TX channel to compensate for transmission delays. The firmware also offers a set of AT-commands to configure the BlueSMiRF v2's settings: including the baud rate, flow control, LED mode, and Over-The-Air (OTA) updates over a 2.4GHz WiFi network to name a few.

    • Serial Bluetooth® Point-to-Point Communication
    • Bluetooth Protocols Supported
      • Serial Port Profile (SPP)
      • Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE)
    • Firmware Over-the-Air (OTA) Updates via 2.4GHz WiFi

    Bluetooth Protocols

    While Bluetooth Classic (Serial Port Profile or "SPP") is the primary communication protocol, which makes it highly compatible with Android devices and most computers, the BlueSMiRF v2 also supports Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) protocol for connecting with Apple devices and other low-power devices.

    Programming the ESP32 in Arduino

    To program the ESP32 Pico-Mini module in the Arduino IDE, users should select the ESP32 > ESP32 Dev Module from the Board Manager list. Users should also be aware that not all of the capabilities, listed above, are available in the Arduino IDE; the Espressif IDF should be utilized for the full capabilities of the ESP32. When programming the module with the Arduino IDE, only the following peripherals are supported:

    • Only two UART interfaces are available.
    • Only one SPI bus is defined.
    • Only one I2C bus is defined.

    For more technical specifications on the I/O pins, you can refer to the schematics, ESP32 Pico-Mini module datasheet, ESP32 SoC datasheet, and documentation for the ESP32 Arduino core.

    Any of the GPIO pins of the ESP32 module can function as digital pins. In the Arduino IDE, users will need to declare the pinMode() function for the pins used in this manner, in the setup of their sketch.

    • Input

      When configured properly, an input pin will be looking for a HIGH or LOW state. This functionality is accessed in the Arduino IDE using the digitalRead(pin) function. By default, in the Arduino IDE, digitalRead() returns a boolean value. Input pins are high impedance and takes very little current to move the input pin from one state to another.

    • Output

      When configured as an output the pin will be at a HIGH or LOW voltage. This functionality is accessed in the Arduino IDE using the digitalWrite(pin, value) function. Output pins are low impedance; this means that they can provide a relatively substantial amount of current to other circuits.

    The ESP32 module provides a 12-bit ADC (Analog Digital Converters) input on eighteen of its I/O pins. This functionality is accessed in the Arduino IDE using the analogRead(pin) function. By default, in the Arduino IDE, analogRead() returns a 10-bit value. To change the resolution of the value returned by the analogRead() function, use the analogReadResolution(bits) function.

    The ESP32 module supports up to sixteen channels of 20-bit PWM (Pulse Width Modulation) outputs on any of its I/O pins. This is accessed in the Arduino IDE using the analogWrite(pin, value) function. By default, in the Arduino IDE, analogWrite() accepts an 8-bit value. To change the resolution of the PWM signal for the analogWrite() function, use the analogWriteResolution(bits) function.

    The ESP32 module supports up to two 8-bit DAC (Digital Analog Converters) outputs. This is accessed in the Arduino IDE using the dacWrite(pin, value); function. By default, in the Arduino IDE, the ESP32 Dev Module board definition supports:

    DAC1 GPIO 25
    DAC2 GPIO 26

    The ESP32 module provides three UART ports. By default, the UART ports for Serial and Serial1 can be accessed through the Arduino IDE using the serial communication class. By default, in the Arduino IDE, the ESP32 Dev Module board definition supports:

    • Serial UART Port

      RX0 GPIO 1
      TX0 GPIO 3

    • Serial1 UART Port

      RX1 GPIO 21
      TX1 GPIO 22

    Tip

    We have noticed that with the ESP32 Arduino core, Serial.available() does not operate instantaneously. This is due to an interrupt triggered by the UART, to empty the FIFO when the RX pin is inactive for two byte periods:

    • At 9600 baud, hwAvailable takes about 11 ms before the UART indicates that data was received from: \r\nERROR\r\n.

      \[ (bytes + 2) * 1ms = 11ms \]

    • At 115200 baud, hwAvailable takes about 1 ms before the UART indicates that data was received from: \r\nERROR\r\n.

      \[ (bytes + 2) * .087ms = 1ms \]

    For more information, please refer to this chatroom discussion.

    The ESP32 module provides three SPI buses. By default, in the Arduino IDE, the SPI class is configured to utilize pins GPIO 32 (SCK), GPIO 25 (POCI), GPIO 26 (PICO), and GPIO 27 (SS) for its chip select. In order to utilize the other SPI ports or objects, users will need to create a custom SPI object and declare which pins to access. By default, in the Arduino IDE, the ESP32 Dev Module board definition supports:

    SCK GPIO 32 (SCK)
    SDI or POCI GPIO 25 (MISO)
    SDO or PICO GPIO 26 (MOSI)
    CS* GPIO 27 (SS)
    Signal Nomenclature

    To comply with the latest OSHW design practices, we have adopted the new SPI signal nomenclature (SDO/SDI and PICO/POCI). The terms Master and Slave are now referred to as Controller and Peripheral. The MOSI signal on a controller has been replaced with SDO or PICO. Please refer to this announcement on the decision to deprecate the MOSI/MISO terminology and transition to the SDO/SDI naming convention.

    The ESP32 module module can support up to two I2C buses. By default, in the Arduino IDE, the Wire class is configured to utilize pins GPIO 20 (SDA) and GPIO 7 (SCL). In order to utilize another I2C port, users will need to create a custom Wire object and declare which pins to access. By default, in the Arduino IDE, the ESP32 Dev Module board definition supports:

    SCL0 GPIO 7 (SCL)
    SDA0 GPIO 20 (SDA)

    Power Pins

    The BlueSMiRF v2 only requires 3.3V and is powered through the VCC and GND pins of its hardware connection.

    Below, is a general summary of the power circuitry:

    • VCC - The input voltage for the BlueSMiRF v2, provided from its hardware connection
      • Supplies the AP2112 LDO regulator
        • Input Voltage Range: 3.3 to 6.0V (1)
        • Powers the ESP32 module, LEDs, and lower voltage of the logic-level shifters
        • The voltage regulator can be bypassed with the JP2 jumper
    • GND - The common ground or the 0V reference for the voltage supplies.
    1. While the input voltage range for the AP2112 LDO voltage regulator is 2.5 to 6.0V, we recommend a minimum input voltage of 3.3V to prevent the ESP32 module browning out.

    UART Interface

    The UART interface provides access to the primary function of the BlueSMiRF v2. It supports full-duplex data transmission for baud rates ranging between 2400-921600bps.

    Device 1 RX TX CTS RTS
    Device 2 TX RX RTS CTS

    Below, is a general summary of the UART interface:

    • Data Bus

      • Features logic-level converters; the higher voltage is supplied by VCC
      • Full-duplex support (simultaneous/bidirectional communication)
      • RXI - Data input to the BlueSMiRF v2 from its hardware connection
        • If connected, data received from the RXI pin is piped to its Bluetooth connection
        • Buffer Size: 51kB
      • TXO - Data output from the BlueSMiRF v2 to its hardware connection
        • If connected, data received from the Bluetooth connection is piped to its TXO pin
        • Buffer Size: 10kB

      Info

      By default, the baud rate is configured to 115200bps. Use the AT-SerialSpeed command to change this setting.

    • Flow Control (Optional)

      • Direct connection to GPIO; no logic-level conversion
      • Does not support legacy hardware flow control for DTE (Data Terminal Equipment) and DCE (Data Communications Equipment)
      • CTS - Connected device, controls data transmission from BlueSMiRF v2
        • HIGH: Connected device, signaling that it is ready for data to be sent
        • LOW: Connected device, signaling that the data transmission needs to be halted
      • RTS - Controls data transmission to the BlueSMiRF v2
        • HIGH: BlueSMiRF v2 is ready for data to be transmitted to it
        • LOW: BlueSMiRF v2 buffer is full; asserts LOW to halt data transmission

      Info

      By default, the flow control pins are disabled in the firmware. Use the AT-FlowControl=1 command to enable them.

      Tip

      The flow control pins aren't necessary for the BlueSMiRF v2 to function. However, when enabled, it assists with buffer control, device latency, and data synchronization.

    Tip

    These data bus and flow control signals need to be cross-coupled between the two devices.

    Pair Button

    The PAIR button is used to switch between the various modes of operation for the BlueSMiRF v2. It is used to simplify pairing, triggering various modes of operation, and restoring the settings to their factory defaults.

    • Discoverable Mode
      • A default state, where the STATUS LED will fade in-and-out a few times to indicate the device is ready to be paired with a Bluetooth device.
    • BlueSMiRF Pairing Mode
      • Used to pair the BlueSMiRF v2 with another BlueSMiRF v2 in the (default) discoverable mode.
      • Press and hold the PAIR button down for 4 - 8sec, the CONNECT and STATUS LED will blink back-and-forth.
        • If the PAIR button is continued to be held, the device will transition to a factory reset (see below).
      • If the BlueSMiRF v2 hasn't been discovered or paired with the other BlueSMiRF v2 after a few seconds, it will return to the (default) discoverable mode and the STATUS LED will fade in-and-out a few times to indicate the transition.
    • Bootloader Mode
      • Used to manually update the firmware through the UART interface.
      • To enter bootloader mode, users will need to hold down the PAIR button while the BlueSMiRF v2 is not powered.
      • Continue holding down the PAIR button, while applying power to put the microcontroller into bootloader mode.
      • Release the button, to upload new firmware.
    • Factory Reset
      • Press and hold the PAIR button down for more than 8sec, the CONNECT and STATUS LED will blink back-and-forth, rapidly.
      • Once the user releases the button, the original factory settings are be applied and the BlueSMiRF v2 resets. Then, it returns to the (default) discoverable mode.

    LEDs

    The BlueSMiRF v2 has two status LEDs, which function differently based on the Bluetooth connection and/or datastream status; in conjunction with the device's AT-LedStyle configuration setting and operation mode:

    • Normal Operation

      Setting: AT-LedStyle=0 (Default)
      • CONNECT - Indicates the Bluetooth connection status
        • Discoverable Mode: This LED will blink, while the BlueSMiRF v2 is available to be paired with.
        • Active Connection: This LED will turn solid, once the BlueSMiRF v2 has an active Bluetooth connection
      • STATUS - Indicates the status of the UART datastream
        • Data Transmission: The STATUS LED will blink, as data is sent to the BlueSMiRF v2.
      Setting: AT-LedStyle=1
      • CONNECT - Indicates the Bluetooth connection status and the status of the TXO datastream
        • Discoverable Mode: This LED will blink, regularly, while the BlueSMiRF v2 awaits for an active Bluetooth connection.
        • Active Connection
          • Initial Connection: Upon an initial connection, this LED will remain solid until data is passed through the TXO pin of the BlueSMiRF v2.
          • Data Transmission: This LED will blink as data is passed through the TXO pin of the BlueSMiRF v2.
      • STATUS - Indicates the status of the RXI datastream
        • Data Transmission: This LED will blink, as data is passed through the RXI pin of the BlueSMiRF v2; regardless of the Bluetooth connection status.

    • Pairing Mode

      • After the PAIR button has been held down for 4-8sec, the CONNECT and STATUS LED will blink back-and-forth.
        • If the PAIR button is continued to be held, the device will transition to a factory reset (see below).
      • After a few seconds in pairing mode, if the BlueSMiRF v2 hasn't been paired with, it will return to the (configured) discoverable mode and the STATUS LED will fade in-and-out a few times to indicate the transition.
    • Factory Reset
      • After the PAIR button has been held down past 8sec sec, the CONNECT and STATUS LED will blink back-and-forth, rapidly.
      • Once the user releases the button, the original factory settings are be applied and the BlueSMiRF v2 resets. Then, it returns to the (default) discoverable mode.
    • Command Mode
      • Upon entering command mode, the CONNECT and STATUS LED will blink simultaneously.

      • After an AT-command has been initially sent sent:

        Setting: AT-LedStyle=0 (Default)

        The LEDs will then return to functioning in the (default) normal operation.

        Setting: AT-LedStyle=1

        The LEDs will continue to blink simultaneously.

    Reset Pin

    The reset pin for the ESP32 is connected to the RST PTH pad. Pulling this pin LOW will restart the ESP32.

    Tip

    This is primarily provided for our production team, as a test point for programming and testing the board. However, users can use it when reprogramming the ESP32 microcontroller.

    Button Operation

    The PAIR button on the BlueSMiRF v2 is used to enter a few different states:

    • Discoverable Mode
      • A default state, where the device is ready to be paired with a Bluetooth device.
    • BlueSMiRF Pairing Mode
      • Used to pair the BlueSMiRF v2 with another BlueSMiRF v2 in the (default) discoverable mode.
    • Bootloader Mode
      • Used to manually update the firmware through the UART interface.
    • Factory Reset
      • Forces the BlueSMiRF v2 to return to its original factory settings. Then, it returns to the (default) discoverable mode.

    Create a Serial Link

    In its default configuration, users can simply scan for and connect to the BlueSMiRF v2, from their phone or a laptop using a Bluetooth connection. However, for a serial bridge between two BlueSMiRF v2 boards, one board needs to be in discoverable mode, before the other device enters pairing mode and creates their connection.

    1. Device 1: By default, the device be in discoverable mode and be available for pairing.
      • Device 1 will broadcast the name BlueSMiRF-Pairing and wait for an incoming connection.
    2. Device 2: Hold the PAIR button for 4 to 8 seconds, then release.
    3. Device 2: Will then enter pairing mode and scan for any compatible devices to pair with.
      • The scan should find that Device 1 is in discoverable mode broadcasting the BlueSMiRF-Pairing name.
    4. Device 2: Connects to Device 1
      • Device 2 stores the MAC address of Device 1
      • Whenever Device 2 boots up, it will always try to connect to the stored the MAC address (i.e. Device 1).
    5. Device 1: Remains agnostic of any connections or pairing; no MAC address has been stored on it.
      • When Device 1 boots, it will not attempt to connect to any devices.

    Note

    The BlueSMiRF that was detected (Device 1), does not store the MAC address of the pairing BlueSMiRF (Device 2). Upon restart, the BlueSMiRF (Device 1) will return to the (default) discoverable mode.

    Discoverable Mode

    A default state, where the device will broadcast the name BlueSMiRF-Pairing, blink the Connect LED slowly, and wait for an incoming connection.

    To enter discoverable mode:

    1. With no other BlueSMiRF devices in discoverable mode, hold the PAIR button down
    2. Wait (~4-8 seconds) until the Status and Connect LEDs to slowly blink back and forth
    3. Release the PAIR button
    4. The BlueSMiRF v2 will enter pairing mode and scan for available devices it can connect to
    5. After a few seconds, if it has failed to connect with another device, the board will return to its discoverable mode
      • In this mode, the Connect LED will slowly blink

    Note

    When the BlueSMiRF's connection is paired by another device, it does not store the MAC address of the connecting device; nor will it attempt to restore that connection upon restart.

    BlueSMiRF Pairing Mode

    Pairing mode is used to scan and connect to other BlueSMiRF v2 devices in discoverable mode.

    To enter pairing mode:

    1. Hold the PAIR button down
    2. Wait (~4-8 seconds) until the Status and Connect LEDs to slowly blink back and forth
    3. Release the PAIR button

    Once a BlueSMiRF has entered pairing mode, it will scan for other compatible BlueSMiRF devices broadcasting the BlueSMiRF-Pairing name. If a device is detected, it will connect to the device and store the MAC address of the located device so that it can reconnect to the same device in the future. Otherwise, if no device is found after a few seconds, the BlueSMiRF will return to its discoverable mode.

    Bootloader Mode

    Hold down the PAIR button while the BlueSMiRF v2 is not powered. Applying power will set the microcontroller to bootloader mode; the STATUS LED should also be dimmed. After uploading the firmware binaries to the ESP32, you will need to cycle power to the device, as it does not automatically hard reset.

    Tip

    To automatically enable a hard reset, users can connect the DTR pin of USB-to-serial converter to the RST PTH pad of the BlueSMiRF with a IC Hook with Pigtail.

    Factory Reset

    Holding the PAIR button for more than 8 seconds will cause the Status and Connect LEDs to quickly blink back and forth. If the PAIR button is released at this time, the device settings will be restored to their factory defaults.

    Configuration Settings

    Command Mode

    Similar to the previous versions of the BlueSMiRF, all communication with the device is retransmitted between its Bluetooth serial link and UART interface. To configure the device, you must send a command key within the first 60 seconds after power-on or reset. This will trigger the device to pause the datastream and enter command mode, where the device settings can be configured using a set of AT-commands.

    Command Key

    • UART Interface: $$$
    • Bluetooth Serial Link: +++ (remote access)

    Tip

    The settings of the command key can be reconfigured with the following commands:

    • AT-BluetoothEscapeCharacter
    • AT-EscapeCharacter
    • AT-EscapeCharacterCount
    • AT-MaxCommandTimeMs
      • By default, the command key can only be sent during the first 60 seconds after powering or resetting the device.

    Once the BlueSMiRF v2 recognizes the command key, it responds with OK; and the CONNECT and STATUS LEDs will blink together. AT-commands must be sent one at a time, each ending with a carriage return (Enter). The device will echo the AT-command and return OK on success, or ERROR if the input is invalid.

    Note

    Depending on your serial terminal's settings, you may not see the characters that are being sent. You can try turning on the local echo to view the characters. However, keep in mind that any characters sent when the BlueSMiRF v2 is in command mode will already be echoed back.

    Firmware Overview

    The BlueSMiRF v2 comes pre-programmed with the latest version of our open-source firmware. However, users are free to modify the firmware and/or reprogram their board for their purposes.

    Our firmware provides short-range, point-to-point communication; allowing serial UART data to be transmitted between two Bluetooth devices. It includes support for both the SPP and BLE protocols and features a massive >10kB buffer for each RX and TX channel to compensate for transmission delays. The firmware also offers a set of AT-commands to configure the BlueSMiRF v2's settings: including the baud rate, flow control, LED mode, and Over-The-Air (OTA) updates over a 2.4GHz WiFi network to name a few.

    • Serial Bluetooth® Point-to-Point Communication
    • Bluetooth Protocols Supported
      • Serial Port Profile (SPP)
      • Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE)
    • Firmware Over-the-Air (OTA) Updates via 2.4GHz WiFi

    Bluetooth Protocols

    While Bluetooth Classic (Serial Port Profile or "SPP") is the primary communication protocol, which makes it highly compatible with Android devices and most computers, the BlueSMiRF v2 also supports Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) protocol for connecting with Apple devices and other low-power devices.

    Manually Reprogramming

    If for some reason the OTA method can't be used, users can also manually reprogram the microcontroller on the BlueSMiRF v2.

    1. Download the Firmware: Download and unzip the contents of the SparkFun_BlueSMiRF-v2_Binaries GitHub repository.
      Download BlueSMiRF v2 Binaries GitHub Repo (ZIP)

    2. Connect Hardware: Wire the BlueSMiRF v2 to a USB-to-Serial converter, ensuring the TX and RX connections are crossed over. To automatically trigger a reset, connect the converter's DTR pin to the BlueSMiRF v2's RST PTH pad.

      USB-to-Serial Converter BlueSMiRF v2
      DTR RST (PTH Pad)
      RXI TXO
      RXI TXO
      3V3 VCC
      GND GND

    3. Identify the Port: Find your USB-to-Serial COM port using Windows Device Manager or your macOS/Linux terminal (e.g., /dev/ttyUSB0).

    4. Upload the Firmware: Open a command prompt or terminal, navigate to the unzipped firmware directory, and execute the appropriate flash command for your OS:

      • Windows: Run the following command in the command prompt 
        .\batch_program.bat .\<firmware_name.bin> <COM_PORT>
      • macOS/Linux: Run the following command in the terminal 
        ./batch_program.sh ./<firmware_name.bin> <COM_PORT>
      Script Details

      These scripts basically run the following command in a loop:

      <ESPTOOL_EXECUTABLE> --chip esp32 --port <COM_PORT> --baud 921600 --before default-reset --after hard-reset write-flash -z --flash-mode dio --flash-freq 80m --flash-size 8MB 0x1000 bins/bootloader.bin 0x8000 bins/partitions.bin 0xe000 bins/boot_app0.bin 0x10000 <BINARY_FILE>

      Where the the following are variables are replaced with:

      • Windows:
        • <ESPTOOL_EXECUTABLE>: esptool.exe
        • <COM_PORT>: COM# (i.e. COM4)
      • Linux/MacOS:
        • <ESPTOOL_EXECUTABLE>: esptool
        • <COM_PORT>: /dev/tty* (i.e. /dev/ttyUSB0)
      • <BINARY_FILE>: BlueSMiRF_Firmware_vx_x.bin

      Tip

      Some users have reported issues, where the 921600 baud rate does not work. If you encounter this problem, reduce the baud rate to 115200.

    5. Start the Programming Process: When prompted, press a keyboard key to start the programming process.

    6. Enter Bootloader Mode: When the Connecting....... prompt pops up, press and hold the PAIR button.

      Tip

      Bootloader mode is active when the STATUS LED is dimly lit and the CONNECT LED is off.

    7. Wait for Process to Complete

    8. Reboot: Power cycle the BlueSMiRF by disconnecting and reconnecting power to exit bootloader mode and run the new firmware.

      Tip

      If you previously connected the DTR pin of the USB-to-Serial converter to the RST PTH pad on the BlueSMiRF v2, it isn't necessary to power cycle the board.

    9. Programming Complete:: Wait for the following prompt Done programming! Ready for next board.

    10. Next:

      • To exit, click Ctrl + C to exit the batch file. You can also just close the window.
      • Otherwise, connect another BlueSMiRF v2 to the USB-to-serial converter and click a keyboard key to program the next board

    To distinguish between the two BlueSMiRFs, try labeling the two board. In this case, we chose had the one with male headers and the other one with female headers.

    Connect the first BlueSMiRF v2 to your computer's COM port. In this case, it was COM13. Press and hold the PAIR button between 4 to 8 seconds. The Status and Connect LEDs will slowly blink back and forth. The Connect LED will begin to fade in and out after a few seconds. Open a serial terminal and connect to the first BlueSMiRF at 115200 baud.

    At this point, connect the second BlueSMiRF v2 to your computer's COM port. In this case, it was COM18. Press and hold the second BlueSMiRF's PAIR button between 4 to 8 seconds. The second BlueSMiRF will scan the area for another BlueSMiRF and connect. Once connected, the Connect LEDs on both boards will remain solid. Open a second serial terminal, and connect to the second BlueSMiRF at 115200 baud. Move both serial terminals side by side.

    Start typing characters in the first serial terminal. You should see them in the second serial terminal!

    Type a message in the second terminal window. You should see them in the first serial terminal!

    In this example, we will be using the basic serial UART example from the SerLCD tutorial. However, the wires between the SparkFun RedBoard will be replaced with a pair of BlueSMiRF v2s.

    Changing the Baud Rate

    Before using the BlueSMiRFs with the SerLCD, we need to make sure that the baud rates match. You can either change the baud rate on both BlueSMiRFs or the baud rate of the SerLCD. To keep the original Arduino code the same, let's change the baud rate on both of the BlueSMiRFs. This will also be a good exercise to change the baud rate using the AT Commands.

    Connect a USB-to-Serial converter to the BlueSMiRF. Depending on the BlueSMiRF that you have, you may need to solder headers. Then connect the USB cable between the converter and your computer.

    Open a serial terminal and connect to the COM port at 115200 baud. Type $$$ within 60 seconds of powering the BlueSMiRF to enter command mode.

    Type AT-SerialSpeed=9600 to change the baud rate to match the SerLCD's baud rate (which is set to 9600 baud by default). Enter ATW to save the settings.

    Now that the first BlueSMiRF v2 is configured, repeat the same steps explained above to change the baud rate of the second BlueSMiRF v2.

Since the BlueSMiRF v2 includes logic level circuitry on the TX and RX pins, we can wire the SparkFun RedBoard Plus directly to the pin when the switch for the microcontroller's logic level is set to 5V.  OpenLCD is an LCD with serial/I2C/SPI interfaces.
 By: Nathan Seidle
 SparkFun Electronics
 Date: April 19th, 2015
 License: This code is public domain but you buy me a beer if you use this and we meet someday (Beerware license).

 This example shows how to display a counter on the display over serial. We use software serial because if 
 OpenLCD is attached to an Arduino's hardware serial port during bootloading  it can cause problems for both devices.

 To get this code to work, attached an OpenLCD to an Arduino Uno using the following pins:
 RX (OpenLCD) to Pin 7 (Arduino)
 VIN to 5V
 GND to GND

*/

#include <SoftwareSerial.h>

SoftwareSerial OpenLCD(6, 7); //RX, TX

byte counter = 0;

void setup()
{
  Serial.begin(9600); //Start serial communication at 9600 for debug statements
  Serial.println("OpenLCD Example Code");

  OpenLCD.begin(9600); //Start communication with OpenLCD
}

void loop()
{
  //Send the clear command to the display - this returns the cursor to the beginning of the display
  OpenLCD.write('|'); //Setting character
  OpenLCD.write('-'); //Clear display

  OpenLCD.print("Hello World!    Counter: "); //For 16x2 LCD
  //OpenLCD.print("Hello World!        Counter: "); //For 20x4 LCD
  OpenLCD.print(counter++);

  delay(250); //Hang out for a bit
}

    FAQ

    • Can the BlueSMiRF v2 be used to program another Arduino board through its serial interface?
      • No, we recommend removing the BlueSMiRF v2 and using a USB-to-serial adapter instead
    • Can the BlueSMiRF v2 be used to update the firmware of a GNSS module on our breakout boards?
      • No, we recommend using a USB-to-serial adapter instead

    Device Compatibility

    To be directly compatible with the BlueSMiRF v2, devices need the hardware connections listed below. Otherwise, our JST pigtail cables and jumper wires provide more flexibility for devices with a proper power supply, logic-levels, and a UART interface.

    BlueSMiRF/Serial Interface Pins

    To connect the BlueSMiRF v2 directly, a compatible device needs to have a 0.1" spaced header, with a pin layout of GND/NC/VCC/TX/RX/NC:

    JST Cables

    To utilize our 4-pin to 6-pin JST-GH cables, a compatible device needs to have a 4-pin JST connector with a pin layout of VCC/TX/RX/GND, from left-to-right, as viewed looking into the connector:

    Flow Control Pins

    Only connect 3.3V signals to the flow control pins of the UART interface; there is no logic-level conversion for these pins. When utilizing a UART-to-USB adapter with flow-control enabled, the RTS and CTS pins should be connected between the two devices.

    Reprogramming the BlueSMiRF v2

    If you are seeing the following error when attempting to manually reprogram the firmware:

    A fatal error occurred: Failed to connect to ESP32: No serial data received. For troubleshooting steps visit: https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esptool/en/latest/troubleshooting.md_in_html

    Double-Check:

    • The wires are making connection and not loose
    • The wiring for USB-to-Serial Converter is connected properly the BlueSMiRF v2
    • The proper COM port is utilized for the USB-to-Serial converter, as enumerated to on your computer
    • The appropriate USB drivers ar installed for the USB-to-Serial converter
    • The BlueSMiRF v2 is in Bootloader mode

    Still having issues? Try a power cycle by following the steps below:

    1. Disconnect the USB-to-serial converter from your computer
    2. Hold down on the PAIR button, to force the BlueSMiRF v2 into bootloader mode
    3. While holding the PAIR button, reconnect the USB-to-serial converter back to your computer
    4. Release the PAIR button

    Baud Rate

    Some users have reported issues, where the 921600 baud rate does not work. If you encounter this problem, reduce the baud rate to 115200.
